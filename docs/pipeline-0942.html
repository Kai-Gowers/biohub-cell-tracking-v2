How a 3D movie of an embryo becomes a family tree of cells, second edition

A microscope films an embryo growing: a stack of slices, about 100 times over. The job is to find every cell nucleus in every 3D frame and say which nucleus it was one frame earlier. This page walks through the pipeline on the replicate-0942 branch, a faithful port of a public solution that scores 0.942 on the leaderboard. Plain version first, machine-learning detail underneath, and at the end what changed from the previous edition of this page.

Stage 0

The data and the goal

Each training video is a 3D movie of a growing embryo whose nuclei glow. Scientists have hand-marked a sample of nuclei and drawn the links between them. The pipeline has to mark all of them and draw all the links. It is graded on how many of the hand-drawn links it reproduces within a 7 micrometre tolerance.

199training videos, each 40 to 100 frames of about 64 × 256 × 256 voxels
133,318hand-annotated nuclei across all videos (a sparse sample, not every cell)
128,883annotated parent → child links, of which only 151 are divisions
7 µmhow close a predicted nucleus must be to a hand-marked one to count as the same cell

Voxels are not cubes, and units matter

  • A raw voxel measures 0.406 × 0.406 µm in y and x but 1.625 µm in z: the microscope slices are four times coarser than the pixels within a slice.
  • Three coordinate systems live in the code: raw voxels (z, y, x) on disk and in the submission; the isotropic model grid the networks see; and micrometres, where every distance rule in the repair stage is written. The node transformer is the exception: it sees displacements in raw voxels divided by 100, because that is how the public weights were trained.
  • Ground truth is sparse. Roughly 98% of the links the pipeline predicts touch a cell nobody annotated, and the metric simply ignores those. Over-linking is nearly invisible locally; missing an annotated link is not.
  • Only two embryos. Names are {embryo}_{field of view}, and every training video comes from embryo 44b6 (71 videos) or 6bba (128). The hidden test videos are from other embryos. Every checkpoint scored so far does markedly worse on the crowded 44b6, and the gap is mostly wrong links between close neighbours.
  • The public weights saw every training video. The shipped checkpoints were trained on all 199 videos, so any number they produce on our 20-video held-out split is a training-set number. Honest held-out numbers will come from the two seeds now training on 179 videos.

Stage 1

Prepare each frame

Every video is re-scaled once, using two brightness levels the dataset already stores for it, so that its dim end lands at 0 and its bright end near 1. Then each frame is shrunk four times in y and x by simply keeping every fourth voxel, so each remaining voxel is a cube. Nothing is averaged and nothing is clipped at the top; a very bright nucleus stays brighter than 1.

Why this is the only path from disk to network

  • prepare_frame() is called by both the training sampler and the predictor. If normalisation ever differed between them, the detection threshold in Stage 3 would silently stop meaning what it meant during training.
  • The quantiles come from the zarr root attributes the competition ships (image_statistics.quantiles). A volume without them raises rather than falling back to a guess.
  • Zarr volumes are read frame by frame as raw blosc2 chunks, because Kaggle submission notebooks run without internet and without a full array library.
  • The decimated uint16 cache (about 10 GB for all videos) is purely a speed trick for training. Inference reads the zarr directly.

Stage 2

Detector: a temporal U-Net that looks at two frames

A neural network looks at the current frame and the next one together. It squeezes each image down to see the big picture, lets every spot in one frame consult the same spot in the other frame, then expands back up. It produces two things per frame: a probability that a cell centre sits at each voxel, and a 32-number description of every voxel that the link scorer will reuse in Stage 4. The same network is run twice with two independently trained sets of weights.

Architecture

  • TemporalUNet3D: three stages of 32, 64 and 128 channels, blocks of Conv3d → BatchNorm → ReLU twice, max-pool down and trilinear upsample up, skip connections by concatenation. Ported verbatim from the public support pack (RoyerLab baseline code).
  • Temporal attention: the tensor is reshaped so each spatial location is a 2-token sequence (frame t, frame t + 1), and standard 4-head self-attention runs over those two tokens with a residual add. It is skipped at full resolution, where it would dominate memory and runtime for little gain.
  • Two heads on one trunk. The U-Net's 32-channel output is both the detection feature map (through a 1×1×1 convolution to one logit) and the per-node feature source for the node transformer. There is no dropout in the U-Net.
  • Two seeds. The primary weights are the pack's split_0 checkpoint (best of 400 epochs by its own validation); the secondary is an independent run with seed 314159, same code and recipe. Both are loaded and run on every window; how their outputs are combined is Stage 3 and Stage 4.

Stage 3

Find the peaks: eight views, two seeds, one list of points

The network's output is a cloud of probabilities, not a list of cells. To steady that cloud, each frame is shown to the network eight times, rotated and mirrored in the flat plane, and the answers are averaged. The second network's average is then blended in, but only if the blend does not lose too many peaks compared with the first network alone. Finally, a voxel becomes a cell if it is very confident and the highest in its 3×3×3 neighbourhood.

Details that matter

  • Averaging is done on logits, not probabilities. The eight views are identity, three flips, two 90° rotations, transpose and anti-transpose; z is never touched because a z-flip is not a symmetry of a 1.625 µm-sliced stack.
  • The same eight views also average the 32-channel feature map (Stage 4's input) for the primary model, and are blended at 75% into the secondary's feature map. This "edge-feature TTA" is one of six runtime patches the public notebook applies on top of the pack's script.
  • Moment matching shifts and rescales the secondary's logits to the primary's mean and standard deviation, with the scale ratio clamped between 0.5 and 2, so two networks with differently calibrated logits can be averaged meaningfully.
  • On the held-out set the combined detector finds about 98% of annotated nuclei within 7 µm, and dual seed is worth about +0.010 in score over the primary alone.

Stage 4

Score links with a node transformer

Every detected cell in frame t and every detected cell in frame t + 1 is described by the detector's 32 numbers at its position plus a fixed encoding of where it is. A small transformer lets the cells in one frame look at all the cells in the other frame, four times back and forth, so each cell's description absorbs its surroundings. Then every possible pair is scored, and for each cell in frame t + 1 the scores over its possible parents are turned into probabilities that sum to one. The same is done in reverse time and with the second network, and the three answers are fused.

Why softmax over parents, and what the fusions do

  • Softmax over sources per target encodes the rule "every child has exactly one parent" directly into the probabilities, which is the right prior for cells that do not divide (the vast majority). A parent with two children simply appears strongly in two columns.
  • Bidirectional harmonic fusion runs the same transformer with the frames swapped, transposes the result, and combines the two probability tables with a weighted harmonic mean (0.85 forward, 0.15 reverse). A harmonic mean is dragged down by either side being small, so a link both directions like survives and a one-sided link is suppressed.
  • Low-margin consensus looks at each child's top two parent probabilities from the primary. If the margin is under 0.35 and the secondary model picks the same top parent, the secondary's (moment-matched) logits are mixed in with weight up to 0.20, scaled by how uncertain the primary was. If they disagree, the secondary is ignored for that child.
  • The displacement fed to the pair MLP is in raw voxels divided by 100, so z and y/x are not on the same physical scale. This is faithful to how the public weights were trained and is not a bug to fix.
  • Held-out association recall is about 98% overall but drops to 92% for nuclei with a neighbour closer than 7 µm; that crowding is where 44b6's remaining errors live.

Stage 5

Select links: an integer linear program

Now every plausible pair has a probability. Rather than grabbing the best pairs one at a time, the pipeline hands the whole video to an exact optimiser. It must give every cell at most one parent and at most two children, and it is charged a price for every cell that vanishes without a child and a smaller price for every division, while being rewarded for every confident link it keeps. It finds the cheapest overall answer for the whole video at once.

What the solver is really contributing

  • The graph is built in tracksdata with the candidate edges and their edge_prob; the solver is ILPSolver with the four weights at right, backed by SCIP through ilpy, all installed offline from the pack's wheels.
  • The disappearance cost of 2.0 is the strongest term. It makes the solver keep tracks alive through weak links rather than end them, and it drops detections that cannot be linked at all. Together with the short-track filter in Stage 6 this pushes the predicted node count below the annotated count (ratio about 0.9), which the metric's node-count penalty does not punish.
  • The next stage discards the ILP's edges and re-derives them from geometry plus these probabilities. What survives from this stage is the node set and the probability lookup table, so the ILP acts mainly as a principled detection filter.
  • Measured: stopping here (no Stage 6) scores 0.9182 on the held-out split versus 0.8837 with greedy selection in place of the ILP.

Stage 6

Repair the graph: nine passes from the public notebook

The solver's answer is then reworked by a chain of rules. First all the links are redrawn using where each cell was heading, with the network's probability as a tie-breaker. Then short breaks in tracks are bridged, by inventing a cell where one frame was missed and checking the raw image really has something there. A few clear divisions are added. Very short tracks are thrown away unless they look trustworthy, and finally each track is smoothed. Every rule has a cap so it cannot run away.

How to read the effect of this stage

  • On our local metric, this stage is neutral to slightly negative: 0.9175 with it versus 0.9182 stopping after the ILP. It adds about 127 false-positive and 34 false-negative edges and recovers three divisions. The notebook's authors saw the same sign flip between their sparse local proxy and the dense leaderboard, so this is not a reason to drop it; it means our local metric cannot rank post-processing variants.
  • Consequently the rule on this branch: keep the full chain for submissions, rank models by the ILP-stage number.
  • DeepCenter is a separate GroupNorm/SiLU 3D U-Net (24 base channels) trained to paint nucleus centres on a 4× mean-pooled, per-frame percentile-normalised frame. The checkpoint is its epoch 2 of 500, as shipped; the port checks the epoch on load. It is consulted only for gap-closing nodes with span ≥ 8.5 µm.
  • Every pass is a boolean in PostprocessConfig and can be switched off from the command line with --post-off for an ablation. The whole stage was verified bit-exact against the notebook's own cell on two held-out videos.

Stage 7

Write the graph and score it

The points and links for each video are saved as a small graph file, then flattened into the competition's CSV. To grade it, every predicted point is matched to the nearest hand-marked point within 7 micrometres, and the links are compared. Links that touch a cell nobody annotated are simply skipped, not penalised. A tenth of the score comes from getting divisions right.

Files, parity, and honest numbers

  • predict.py writes one .geff per video, carrying each edge's probability so the graph stays inspectable; make_submission.py converts them to submission.csv. On Kaggle the run notebook installs the ILP stack from an attached wheels dataset, checks every model file's sha256, and audits the CSV.
  • Parity is the contract. The pack's own script, with and without the notebook's six patches, was rerun on two held-out videos, and our ILP-stage output matched it node for node, edge for edge, with zero difference in probabilities. Our post-processing likewise matched the notebook's cell exactly. Any change to the predictor, the ILP or the repair stage reruns this check.
  • Read the numbers below as training-set numbers. The public weights trained on all 199 videos, including our 20 held-out ones. They are still informative for same-weights, different-configuration comparisons, which is how every ablation on this page was measured.
  • Cross-checkpoint local comparisons were anti-correlated with the leaderboard in the sibling repository. Per-embryo numbers are always reported; the leaderboard is the only true cross-embryo signal.

Training

The 400-epoch recipe, now running on our own split

The detector and the link scorer are trained at the same time from the same batches. Each step takes eight two-frame windows, randomly mirrors them in any of the three axes and nudges their brightness, and pushes the detector to light up at the hand-marked nuclei. On the same step, the detector's fresh detections are matched to the hand-marked cells so the link scorer can be told which pairs are truly the same cell. This runs for 400 passes over the data at a fixed learning rate; on one of this cluster's GPUs that is about a week per seed.

The recipe, our additions, and where it stands

  • The recipe is the pack's, verbatim: AdamW at 1e-4 with default weight decay, batch 8, gradient clip 1.0, brightness shift ±0.1, independent z/y/x flips, 400 epochs. The pack's own history reached its best validation at epoch 381 of 400 on an RTX 5090, about 20 minutes per epoch.
  • What we added around it: resumable checkpoints, a seed, periodic snapshots, a wall-clock limit for the self-resubmitting SLURM chain, and val_score, the competition metric computed through the ILP stage (no TTA) every 5 epochs, which selects the _best checkpoint. The pack's own accuracy × recall validation is logged but not used for selection.
  • Held-out discipline: train on 179 videos, hold out 20 (5 × 44b6, 15 × 6bba). The pack's "train on all 199, validate on 40 of them" is deliberately not reproduced, because it is what makes the public weights' held-out numbers training-set numbers.
  • Status on 2026-09-11: two seeds (0 and 314159) are training, one L40S each, about 25 minutes per epoch, so roughly 7 days per seed. DeepCenter is training separately for 50 epochs. Decision point at epoch 100: sweep the epoch 25/50/75/100 snapshots through the full pipeline per embryo, and stop early if the tracking score has flattened.
  • Four-GPU DataParallel was measured at only 1.6× because CPU-side per-sample loops dominate the step, and was rejected. Vectorising those loops is the obvious same-numerics speed-up, not yet done.

Changes

What changed from the first edition of this page

The previous pipeline was this repository's own design: a smaller network, a tiny link scorer, and a greedy linker with no repairs. It reached about 0.80 on held-out videos and 0.842 on the leaderboard. On 11 September it was set aside in favour of replicating a public solution near-verbatim, with attribution. Almost every stage changed; this table is the diff.

What carried over unchanged. The metric implementation, the held-out split of 20 whole videos, per-embryo reporting, the error-analysis harness, the frame-by-frame zarr reader, the SLURM batch runner, and the habit of writing a report for every experiment. The insight that drove the switch also carried over: association errors in crowded regions are the problem, and the previous edge scorer was the smallest part of the previous pipeline.

Glossary

Words used above

voxel
A 3D pixel: one little box of the volume with one brightness value.
µm (micrometre)
A millionth of a metre. A nucleus here is roughly 5 to 10 µm across.
frame
One full 3D snapshot of the embryo. A video is 40 to 100 frames.
quantile
The brightness below which a given fraction of all voxels fall. The 0.999 quantile is brighter than 99.9% of the video.
logit, sigmoid, softmax
The network outputs unbounded numbers (logits); a sigmoid squashes one to a probability, a softmax turns a group of them into probabilities that sum to one.
U-Net
A network shaped like a U: shrink the image to understand context, expand it back to make a per-voxel decision, with shortcuts across.
attention
A mechanism where each item computes weights over other items and mixes in their information. Self-attention: within one set. Cross-attention: from one set to another.
test-time augmentation (TTA)
Running the network on several transformed copies of the input and averaging the answers.
seed
The random starting point of a training run. Two seeds give two different networks trained the same way.
moment matching
Shifting and scaling one set of numbers so its mean and spread equal another's.
harmonic mean
An average that is pulled toward the smaller value. Used to require agreement.
ILP
Integer linear program: an optimisation over yes/no choices with linear costs and constraints, solved exactly.
Hungarian matching
The exact algorithm for pairing two sets so the total cost is smallest.
synthetic node
A cell position the repair stage invents to bridge a frame where the detector found nothing.
DeepCenter
A separate small network that paints where nucleus centres are; used here only to confirm synthetic nodes.
Jaccard index
Overlap between two sets: matches divided by everything in either set. 1.0 means perfect.
held-out
Data the model never trained on, kept back to estimate how it will do on new data.
parity
Producing exactly the same output as the reference code on the same input.
